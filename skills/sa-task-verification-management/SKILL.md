# Software Assurance & Safety Tasks — Management (NASA-STD-8739.8B §4.3 Table 1, NPR Ch.3)

## Overview

Records that software assurance and safety personnel actually performed the confirmation, assessment, and analysis tasks §4.3 Table 1 assigns against each Chapter 3 (Software Management) requirement, and where the evidence for each lives. Does not perform software assurance or safety work itself — that's real engineering and assurance work this tool doesn't replace.

**Announce at start:** "I'm using the sa-task-verification-management skill to record your NASA-STD-8739.8B §4.3 Table 1 software assurance and safety task evidence for NPR Chapter 3."

## Precondition

Requires `docs/nasa-compliance/<subsystem>/sa-task-mapping-matrix.yaml` to already exist with at least one Chapter 3 row for this subsystem's class. This file is generated by `requirements-matrix` alongside the main requirements matrix. If it's absent, or has no rows starting with `3.`, either this class has no applicable Chapter 3 rows or `requirements-matrix` hasn't been (re-)run since this catalog's rows existed — check there first, don't assume the chapter doesn't apply.

## The interview

Ask each group below in turn. For each, ask which of its listed SWE-ids have real, checkable evidence — an assurance assessment, a confirmed audit finding, a review record, a tracked risk or issue — and where that evidence lives. A vague "assurance handled it" answer is not sufficient, same standard `peer-review-record` and `ivv-verification-record` established: the evidence must point at something a human auditor could actually go check. If a SWE-id has no real evidence yet, leave it out of that group's `swe_ids` below rather than recording an unverifiable claim.

1. **Acquisition & plan setup (SWE-034, SWE-013, SWE-033).** Start with acceptance criteria and life-cycle planning: has someone verified the software's acceptance criteria exist and checked them against the guidance in NASA-HDBK-2203? Separately, are the project's life-cycle plans — the security plan among them — actually in place, tailored correctly for this software's class, and is there a written, current assurance plan that follows HDBK-2203's expected content, safety work included? Last, on the acquire-vs-build decision: has the project's chosen path — buying an off-the-shelf product, building or getting the service done in-house, contracting it out, enhancing or reusing something that already exists, or pulling in source code from outside NASA — actually been evaluated, did the applicable engineering, assurance, and safety requirements get pushed down onto every acquisition activity, and were the risks tied to that decision assessed?
2. **Plan tracking & commitments (SWE-037, SWE-036, SWE-024).** Are the checkpoints where the developer's progress comes up for review and audit actually spelled out and written down, and is assurance showing up to take part in those reviews? Separately — is everything the project needs approved, current, and in place: its processes (safety, IV&V, and assurance work included), the documentation plans, the roster of electronic deliverables, and the task list needed for development — and when a deliverable lands, does the government side actually follow through on whatever it owes back, an approval or a review? Finally, on commitments: does the project check its own plans against the governing directive and standard whenever something changes, are corrective actions actually closed out with a documented rationale, and are changes to commitments logged and tracked rather than left as a verbal understanding?
3. **Developer oversight & reporting (SWE-042, SWE-040, SWE-039).** Can NASA reach into the project's generated code electronically, in a form that can actually be edited? Are the project's outputs — its products, traceability data, change-tracking records, nonconformance reports, plus figures tracking how development and management are progressing — likewise reachable by NASA electronically? And more broadly on oversight: does the developer hand the project manager periodic status updates and visibility into how build and test work are going, is integration of the product being watched, are verification activities checked for adequacy, are trade studies and the underlying source data reviewed and assessed, together with review meetings and cross-team technical sessions, does an audit of development practice happen on a cadence that never stretches past two years, are status reports actually produced, is there a running list of assurance-raised discrepancies, risks, issues and concerns, and does the project manager both answer those items and drive them through to closure?
4. **Compliance & matrix maintenance (SWE-125, SWE-121, SWE-139).** Does the project keep one or more requirement-tracking matrices covering every requirement the governing NPR sets out, plus a separate one for the assurance standard's own requirements? Wherever a requirement has been tailored out, does that tailoring carry the sign-off it needs, and is there a distinct tailoring matrix just for the safety and assurance side? Stepping back: do the project's requirements, deliverables, procedures and processes actually line up with what's demanded, given this software's class and how safety-critical it is?
5. **Reuse, COTS & software reuse assurance (SWE-148, SWE-147, SWE-027).** When the project offers something back as a candidate for reuse elsewhere, does the submission include what's expected — a title, a description, a named civil-servant point of contact, which language(s) it's written in, a record of whatever third-party code shows up, with its licensing terms, and release notes? Has the project also weighed its own software's future reuse potential — models, simulations, and the data behind them included — while planning its development work? And for anything the project pulls in from outside — commercial, government, open-source, or otherwise reused — have the necessary boxes been checked: the requirements that piece must satisfy are spelled out, it comes with documentation sufficient for its purpose, ownership and licensing questions have gone through the Center's Intellectual Property Counsel review, there's an adequate plan for keeping it supported long-term, it's been checked and signed off to the same standard the project would hold an in-house component to, and the project has a standing plan to periodically check vendor defect reports against how it's actually being used?
6. **Cost estimation (SWE-174, SWE-151, SWE-015).** Do the project's planning inputs — size and effort figures, milestones, characteristics — actually land in the Center's own repository for measurement data by each major milestone, and do assurance and safety submit their own estimates and parameters into an organization-wide repository the same way? Do the resulting cost estimate(s) hold up against what's expected: they span the whole life cycle, they're grounded in the project's actual attributes, they price in what maturing the underlying technology will cost, they carry an allowance for uncertainty and exposure to risk — cybersecurity threats included — they capture the cost of needed assurance support, and they include other direct costs? And is the count of cost estimates the project owes actually complete, with an assurance estimate in there that separately prices out handling any safety-critical code or data?
7. **Schedule oversight (SWE-046, SWE-018, SWE-016).** When the developer hands over a revised schedule, do the project's own schedules — assurance's and safety's included — get updated to match? Are recurring reports on schedule status, activity, and metrics actually produced and shared, assurance and safety's own schedule activity included, and do schedule problems get tracked all the way to closure? And does the underlying software schedule hold up on its own terms: does it stay coordinated with the project's overall schedule, does it show where operations, the wider system, the hardware, and the code all intersect on their milestones and deliverables, does it reflect the real critical-path dependencies driving software work, does it account for dependencies tied to work on other projects and dependencies that cross program lines, and is there a matching assurance schedule covering assurance's own reviews, reporting, audits, and products?
8. **Training & classification hygiene (SWE-176, SWE-020, SWE-017).** Is there a maintained, current record — for as long as the project runs — of every classification decision, every mapping matrix produced, and the outcome of each independent classification check? Has the software actually been classified, or has an independent classification been concurred with, using the class definitions the governing directive lays out? And on training: has this project's own software training actually been mapped out, tracked, and brought to completion for its people — safety and assurance staff included — and separately, have the safety and assurance personnel themselves completed what they need to competently carry out that work?
9. **SA/safety role & independence (SWE-179, SWE-178, SWE-131, SWE-141, SWE-022).** When IV&V raises an issue or a risk, does the project manager actually respond to it and see it through to closure? Does IV&V, once engaged, get what it needs to run its analysis well — source code, project data, development artifacts, and finished products among them? Where IV&V is required, has an IV&V Project Execution Plan been written, approved, kept up to date, and actually carried out? For the projects that trigger a mandatory IV&V obligation in the first place — Category 1 work, Category 2 work carrying elevated payload risk, or anything a Mission Directorate specifically named for it — is that obligation being met in full? And zooming out: are assurance, safety, and IV&V (wherever it applies) actually being carried out the way the assurance standard and the project's own plan call for?
10. **Safety-critical & mission-critical software (SWE-220, SWE-219, SWE-134, SWE-023, SWE-205).** On complexity and coverage: has Cyclomatic Complexity actually been measured or analyzed for every safety-critical component, does each one sit at or under a value of 15, and where it doesn't, has the project manager or the proper technical authority reviewed and accepted a rationale for going higher? Is full MC/DC test coverage addressed for every safety-critical component, and where full coverage isn't reached, is there a credible, documented explanation for why not? Digging into the implementation itself: working with the project, has each of the following actually been built in — starting the software in a known-safe condition at first boot and every restart, moving cleanly between predefined states, shutting down to a safe condition, requiring two separate operator actions before any override takes effect, refusing out-of-order commands that would otherwise cause a hazard, catching accidental memory corruption and recovering safely, checking the integrity of inputs and outputs, running prerequisite checks ahead of any safety-critical command, making sure no lone event can trigger a hazard by itself, reacting to off-nominal conditions fast enough to head off a hazard, handling errors, and being able to drive the system to a safe state — and has the actual source code been checked against that list at every project milestone, every safety review, each test review, and every code inspection? Have the actual values riding in safety-critical parameter loads, uplinked commands, rule sets, and scripts that shape hazardous outcomes been confirmed through testing? Has the design itself been examined for proper partitioning or isolation of safety-critical elements and data from everything that isn't, does assurance take part in reviews touching safety-critical products, and does this implementation actually hold together with the system-level hazard analysis? Have the identified safety-critical components and data actually put the standard's safety-critical assurance requirements into practice? Finally, on hazard tracking: is there a maintained safety analysis running across the whole development life cycle, does traceability actually connect software requirements to the hazards their software contributes to, and do the hazard reports and hazard analyses correctly identify which software components tie back to system-level hazards and capture every known way software contributes to one?
11. **Auto-generated code & dev practices (SWE-032, SWE-206, SWE-146).** For Class A or Class B software, are both the development side and the assurance side getting audited, is anything the CMMI appraisal turned up in the way of process issues, findings, or risks being looked at, and is the organization doing the acquiring, building, and maintaining actually holding a current CMMI-DEV rating at the level required — 3 for Class A, 2 for Class B, with the usual carve-out for Class B riding on Class D payloads — from a certified appraiser? Where code gets auto-generated, do NASA, engineering, the project, assurance, and IV&V all have electronic access to whatever models, simulations, and data fed that generation? And has the auto-generation approach itself been spelled out, covering things like validating and verifying the generation tools, keeping configuration control over those tools and their inputs and outputs and any later edits, defining how far auto-generated code is allowed to be used, holding the generated code to the same verification and validation bar as anything written by hand, watching actual use against what was planned, and having a policy for hand-editing the generated output?
12. **Cybersecurity, part 1 (SWE-159, SWE-157, SWE-154, SWE-156).** Has testing actually wrapped up for whatever cybersecurity mitigations came out of the vulnerability and weakness analysis, and separately, has someone judged whether that testing — and its results — are actually good enough? For any software that can communicate externally, do its requirements, its design documentation, and the implementation itself guard against unauthorized access the way the space-system protection standard spells out? Are the cybersecurity risks facing both flight and ground software actually identified, with real, managed mitigations behind them rather than just a list? And has a cybersecurity assessment been run on the software's components against Agency policy and the project's own requirements — specifically weighing the exposure that comes from any commercial, government, open-source, or reused pieces?
13. **Cybersecurity, part 2 (SWE-210, SWE-185, SWE-207).** Do requirements actually exist for gathering, reporting on, and holding onto data tied to spotting adversarial activity? Separate from what the developer reports, has the code itself been checked — using either the underlying engineering records or a separate static-analysis run — against the standard the project has set for coding securely, rather than just taking the developer's word for it? And do the project's coding guidelines genuinely bake in secure-coding practices, not just general style conventions?
14. **Bi-directional traceability (SWE-052).** Has the required two-way traceability actually been completed, logged, and kept up to date across the elements the class-specific table calls for? And separately — does that traceability specifically reach any hazard that software plays a part in, not just the standard development chain?

```bash
    record_md_path='<path to the subsystem's sa-task-verification-management.md>',
    swe_ids=[<ids answered above with checkable evidence, e.g. 'SWE-033', 'SWE-013', 'SWE-034'>],
    fields={
        'acquisition_and_plan_setup': '<answer to question 1>',
        'plan_tracking_and_commitments': '<answer to question 2>',
        'developer_oversight_and_reporting': '<answer to question 3>',
        'compliance_and_matrix_maintenance': '<answer to question 4>',
        'reuse_cots_and_reuse_assurance': '<answer to question 5>',
        'cost_estimation': '<answer to question 6>',
        'schedule_oversight': '<answer to question 7>',
        'training_and_classification_hygiene': '<answer to question 8>',
        'sa_safety_role_and_independence': '<answer to question 9>',
        'safety_critical_and_mission_critical': '<answer to question 10>',
        'auto_generated_code_and_dev_practices': '<answer to question 11>',
        'cybersecurity_part_1': '<answer to question 12>',
        'cybersecurity_part_2': '<answer to question 13>',
        'bi_directional_traceability': '<answer to question 14>',
    },

"
```

Only pass ids that actually have checkable evidence for the fields you're filling in this run — you don't need to answer all 14 groups in one pass. Run the script again later as more evidence becomes available; each run appends a new `## Recorded` entry.
